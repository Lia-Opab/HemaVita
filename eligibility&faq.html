FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS


Is there any health condition that if I have, I can still be able to donate?arrow_drop_down
Yes, there is! People often assume they are disqualified when they have certain health conditions when they actually aren't! Some health conditions that if you have, you can still donate to save a life are:
  • High Blood Pressure / Hypertension: CAN donate, provided your blood pressure is under control and stable on the day of donation (even if you take daily medication for it).
  • Diabetes: CAN donate, as long as your blood sugar is well-controlled through diet or oral medications. (If you use insulin, you just need to double-check local clinic guidelines).
  • Past Piercings & Tattoos: CAN donate with conditions. If the tattoo or piercing was done in a licensed, regulated facility, you can often donate immediately or after a short waiting period (usually 3 to 12 months depending on the country).
  • Minor Colds (Recovered): CAN donate, but only if you have been completely symptom-free for at least 48 hours.
What condition do i have to be in, for me to not donate?arrow_drop_down
With certain health conditions / normal conditions, you wouldn't be allowed to donate blood when at the donation centre. These are critical safety rules to protect both the donor and the patient receiving the blood:
  • Pregnancy & Breastfeeding: Cannot donate. Pregnant women need to keep all their iron and blood volume for the baby. After giving birth, they must wait at least 6 months before donating.
  • Low Iron / Anemia: Cannot donate temporarily. If a rapid finger-prick test at the center shows their hemoglobin levels are too low, they will be deferred until their iron levels go back up.
  • Active Infections: Cannot donate. Anyone currently taking antibiotics, running a fever, or experiencing a sore throat/cough must wait until they are fully recovered.
  • Blood-Borne Illnesses: Permanently cannot donate. Anyone who has ever tested positive for HIV, Hepatitis B, or Hepatitis C can never donate blood, because these viruses are transmitted directly through blood transfusions.
  • Recent Major Surgery: Cannot donate temporarily. They must wait anywhere from 3 to 12 months to ensure their body has fully healed and recovered its blood supply.
What is the process?arrow_drop_down
The process is very simple. First, Go to a donation center near you - you can also check out the Centers page to find an available donation center near you.
When you get to the center, a Medical Director will ask you some questions to check if you are eligible or not - if you also want to see if you are eligible before going to the center, you can find that on the left side of the Eligibility & F.A.Q. page.
After you have answered the questions and you are eligible, you will fill out a basic health form, after which the donation process begins which takes only 8 - 10 minutes to complete with a Blood Donation Technician or Medical Laboratory Technician in the background monitoring if everything is going on well.
After you are done donating, a Donor Care Specialist will take care you thorough your recovery before going home. Donors are given a snack (juice, biscuits, or water) to help their body recover. It’s a very important part of the blood donation culture in Ghana.
Does donating blood hurt?arrow_drop_down
You will feel a quick, minor pinch when the needle goes in (similar to a quick scratch), but during the actual donation process, you shouldn't feel any pain at all. The Phlebotomist(a specialized medical professional who draws blood from a donor) and his team(Blood Donation Technician, Donor Care Specialist & Medical Laboratory Technician (MLT) - If there is a medical emergency or a donor feels very dizzy, there is always a Registered Nurse (RN) or a Medical Director (who is a licensed Doctor) managing the clinic in the background to supervise the phlebotomists!) works hard to ensure you are completely comfortable.
How long does the whole process take?arrow_drop_down
The actual blood donation takes only about 8 to 10 minutes. However, the entire visit—including filling out the health form, a mini-physical check, and resting afterward with snacks—takes around 45 to 60 minutes total.
How much blood is taken during a donation?arrow_drop_down
A standard donation is exactly one pint of blood (about 470 ml). This is only about 10% of the average adult's total blood volume, and your body naturally replaces the fluid within 24 to 48 hours!
Can I donate blood on an empty stomach?arrow_drop_down
No, please don't! To prevent dizziness or fainting, you should eat a healthy, low-fat meal within 4 hours of your appointment. It is also incredibly important to drink plenty of water or juice before you arrive.
What should I not do after I am done donating?arrow_drop_down
After donating, avoid heavy lifting or vigorous exercise for the rest of the day. Keep your bandage on for a few hours, drink extra fluids, and avoid alcohol for the next 24 hours.
How often can I donate blood?arrow_drop_down
For standard whole blood donations, you must wait at least 8 weeks (56 days) between donations. This gives your body's red blood cells plenty of time to fully recover.
Will I catch an illness or disease from donating blood?arrow_drop_down
Absolutely not. Every single needle and collection kit used is completely sterile, used only once for you, and then immediately destroyed. There is zero risk of catching any blood-borne disease from donating.
